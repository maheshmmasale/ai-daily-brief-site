Top Storiescompany claim Google DeepMind · 2026-11-10

Introducing AlphaProof Code: a breakthrough in verifying complex software at depth

DeepMind's AlphaProof Code, a multi-LLM agentic framework, formally verified the actual code of the Signal protocol — the first time AI has formally verified a widely deployed cryptographic protocol at this depth, and the first time any method has verified this implementation. Agents guided by human experts translated Signal's security goals into formal specifications, then generated over six million lines of machine-checked Lean proof; humans only had to review about 1% of the codebase. The whole verification took four weeks, work that historically takes expert teams years. DeepMind is open-sourcing the formal verification artifacts.

Why it matters: Formal verification just went from years of expert labor to a four-week agent workflow — elite security assurance could soon be routine for critical software.

Introducing OWE-Bench for Long-Horizon Engineering Projects

Mercor released OWE-Bench (OpenWorldEngineer), a frontier coding benchmark of 26 long-horizon engineering and research tasks across software engineering, physical engineering, machine learning, and scientific computing — from GPU kernels to aircraft design — each graded on unseen cases on a continuous scale. Engineering & Researchcompany claim Baseten · 2026-11-10

How Baseten and Genspark trained a deep research model to outperform Opus 5.5

Baseten Applied Research and Genspark post-trained Qwen3.5-397B into a specialized Deep Research model that beats closed frontier models on Genspark's own quality grader — 0.759, up from 0.612 for the base model, above Opus 5.5 (0.738), Sonnet 5.5 (0.742), GPT-5.6 Sol (0.637), Kimi K3 (0.724), and DeepSeek V4 Pro (0.675). Why it matters: A detailed, reproducible recipe for beating frontier closed models with an open model — and a case study in training research and writing as two different jobs.

Engineering & Researchcompany claim Modal · 2026-11-12

Sandbox resourcing: burst, don't reserve

Modal explains why its Sandboxes burst instead of reserving fixed resources: a typical Sandbox spends more than half its life using under 6% of peak CPU, since coding agents idle while the model reasons and spike only for installs, tests, and builds. With active-resource pricing — billed per second for what you actually use, from a 0.125-CPU / 128 MiB floor — Modal's example workload costs 8.4 cents per Sandbox-hour versus 20.0 cents for a fixed reservation, a 58% saving, with an OOM SLO below 0.0001% per minute for Sandboxes under 8 GiB.

Why it matters: Agent workloads are spiky by nature; Modal's numbers make the case that reservation pricing is the wrong default for them.

How Glean auto routing determines the right model for every task

Glean explains its auto-routing system: about 25% of requests are simple lookups handled by a fast, lightweight path, while harder requests go to task-specific 'experts' — specialist model-and-reasoning combinations for dashboards, exhaustive analysis, data work, or recovery after a bad answer. Admins pick an Economy, Balanced, or Frontier policy.

Why it matters: A rare public look at production model routing with real numbers — including the cases where the expensive model isn't worth it.

Worth Your Timecompany claim OpenAI · 2026-11-10

Work at the Frontier: AI's effect on freelance work

OpenAI's Economic Research Team, with Upwork, studied how AI is reshaping freelance work across 63 matched work categories. Activities common in ChatGPT use lost share on Upwork in 43 of 63 categories — but not uniformly, so this is not a simple substitution story. AI-related postings became 45% more diverse in their task mix (from about 15 equally common tasks to 21), combined tasks that rarely appeared together before, and carried larger fixed-price budgets and more hours — even though posted hourly rates were similar.

Why it matters: The clearest data yet on how AI changes freelance work: not fewer jobs, but broader, bigger, stranger ones.
