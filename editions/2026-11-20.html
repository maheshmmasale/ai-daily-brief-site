NVIDIA Vera Rubin NVL72 on CoreWeave: 10x More Tokens Per Megawatt Than Blackwell

CoreWeave published the first measured silicon performance for NVIDIA's Vera Rubin NVL72 rack, running DeepSeek R1 against Blackwell GB200 NVL72 with the same inference stack (TensorRT-LLM, Dynamo, NVFP4, disaggregated prefill and decode). Why it matters: The first hard numbers on Rubin's efficiency — a 10x perf-per-watt jump would reshape inference economics for reasoning-heavy agentic workloads.

Claude Fable 5.2 solved two open problems in theoretical physics around black holes and quantum gravity, with preprints published. Why it matters: A frontier model doing original, verified theoretical physics — not just assisting researchers, but completing a 20-year-old calculation and finding a proof humans had missed.

Engineering & Researchcompany claim Databricks · 2026-11-19

Genie One Accuracy: Why Curated Agents Need Governed Context

Databricks tested Genie One on a fictional commercial auto insurer (125 questions, answers scored in SQL) to isolate what governed context adds. Tables alone scored 64% accuracy; curated agents and a governed layer (certified Metric Views, Domains, Pages in Unity Catalog) each reached 83% — but were good at different things. Combined, accuracy hit 93–95%, with definition questions at 100% and loss-ratio questions at 93%. The key failure mode without governance: Genie One answered definition questions itself instead of routing to the agent that knew the answer, getting most of them wrong.

Why it matters: A careful, quantified answer to a real enterprise question — curated agents alone aren't enough; the governed definitions have to be reachable on every path the system might take.

Engineering & Researchcompany claim Databricks · 2026-11-19

Why PostgreSQL 19 shipped late

PostgreSQL 19 shipped more than a month past the project's usual mid-September to mid-October window — and Databricks, as a contributor, says the delay came from AI. Around February 2026, LLMs got good enough to find bugs in Postgres's large, old codebase, and the flood followed: CVEs fixed per quarterly update release went from 1–4 to 28 by August 2026, and commits back-patched to the release branch rose 61%. Time that would have gone into reviewing new features went into fixing bugs instead, and a second release candidate was issued so PG19 could ship with the fixes.

Why it matters: A vivid, measured case study of AI changing open-source development itself — bug discovery is now outrunning bug fixing, and even Postgres's release calendar felt it.

Why agent governance starts with observability

LangChain's argument for where agent governance should start: observability. Guardrails and spend limits don't guarantee an agent follows them, so teams need traces and trajectories to verify preventative controls actually work and to power detective controls — online evals, alerts, drift detection — after the fact. The post works through both halves with examples (a refund-splitting agent dodging a limit is a good one), and notes that across 16,000+ production agents on LangSmith, 87% of teams had tracing in place before their first deployment.

Why it matters: A clear framework for the governance conversation every team running agents in production is having right now — and the case that tracing is the prerequisite, not the add-on.
