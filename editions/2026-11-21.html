Top Storiescompany claim OpenAI · 2026-11-20

Combatting adversarial distillation

OpenAI lays out how it detects and disrupts adversarial distillation — bad actors training a second model on a frontier model's outputs to reproduce its capabilities without its safeguards. The post focuses on protecting chain-of-thought: GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol tie protected reasoning to the conversation that produced it, drop reasoning when earlier messages are rewritten, and block older model families from reading it. In internal adaptive red-teaming, the attack success rate for extracting protected reasoning from an earlier request fell from 54.0% on GPT-6 Astra to 0.2% on GPT-6.2 Astra. OpenAI also says it enforces against thousands to tens of thousands of accounts a week in distillation campaigns, and briefed Congress in February on activity indicating DeepSeek was attempting to distill US frontier models.

Why it matters: The first hard numbers on how well distillation defences actually work — 54% to 0.2% — and a rare public account of the enforcement scale behind them.

Top Storiescompany claim Anthropic · 2026-11-20

LLMs are making formal software verification feasible at a much larger scale

Anthropic and Theorem show Claude proving security properties at the assembly level across 123 real-world open-source libraries from Google's OSS-Fuzz programme. Claude proved 239 fuzz-test entry points will never crash on any input under 64 MB, producing about 180 million lines of Rocq proof — a typical proof runs ~600,000 lines for a binary of ~18,000 RISC-V instructions. Along the way it found and patched hundreds of bugs fuzzers would likely never surface, including a counter underflow needing a 20 MB input and a rediscovery of CVE-2026-56132 in libexpat. The proofs, RISC-V and OS semantics are published, and Theorem offers to run the same pipeline for other open-source projects.

Why it matters: Formal verification used to take 20 person-years for one kernel; Claude did 123 libraries in weeks — a credible path to proven security guarantees becoming routine in software development.

Top Storiescompany claim MiniMax · 2026-11-20

Practical cryptanalysis with MiniMax's latest Internal Model

MiniMax's internal research model (MIM) turned theoretical attacks into working breaks across modern cryptography. On CryptanalysisBench Tier 2 it produced the first practical attacks on several NIST candidates with no known practical break — including full-round collisions on Waterfall-256 and Blender-256, and key recovery on post-quantum candidates LOCKER and ROLLO-I in seconds. It also broke peer-reviewed designs: a forgery on CAESAR winner COLM and key recovery on ASIACRYPT 2025's SBC-VOLE from a single signature. On public-key challenge benchmarks it solved 94 McEliece instances, 55 previously unsolved, at 6,180x to 43,400x the speed of prior records. Findings were shared with scheme authors in advance; reasoning traces and proof-of-concept code are released.

Why it matters: A striking demonstration that frontier models can now do original cryptanalysis, not just solve textbook problems — with direct implications for how cryptographic standards get vetted.

Frontier Labs & Big Techcompany claim Hugging Face · 2026-11-20

Welcome Inkling 2 Mini, Cutting-edge Performance for your Local Setup

Thinking Machines Lab's Inkling 2 Mini is a multimodal mixture-of-experts model with ~26B total parameters and only 3B active per token, built for local setups. Its trick is a looped transformer: 12 of its 24 decoder layers run three times per token, so it behaves like a 48-layer model, with cross-pass messages letting each pass refine the previous one. It supports a 1M-token context and image plus text input. Hugging Face reports it leads in 32 of 48 benchmarks against Gemma 4 31B, Muse Glimmer, Qwen3.8-27B and Nemotron 3.5 Super VL, and tops the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index in its class. It ships in transformers, llama.cpp, MLX and SGLang, with a variable-loop research variant that trades passes for speed.

Why it matters: Looping turns a small local model into something that punches well above its parameter count — and the variable-loop variant makes the speed/quality trade-off a dial, not a fixed choice.

Engineering & Researchcompany claim Thinking Machines Lab · 2026-11-20

Defeating Nondeterminism in LLM Inference

Thinking Machines Lab dismantles the usual explanation for why LLM inference isn't reproducible even at temperature 0. The culprit isn't floating-point non-associativity plus concurrency, as commonly assumed — the forward pass is in fact run-to-run deterministic, with no atomic adds in the typical path. The real cause is a lack of batch invariance: a request's output depends on the batch size of the forward pass it lands in, because matmul and attention kernels give different results for the same element at different batch sizes. The post works through the mechanism with experiments and explains how to get truly reproducible results.

Why it matters: Reproducibility is the bedrock of evals, debugging and science — this pins down exactly why your greedy decoding still drifts, and what to do about it.

Engineering & Researchcompany claim Hugging Face · 2026-11-20

Introducing harness adapters: adapt your favorite model to your harness to improve performance and cost

Hugging Face shows that RL training inside your actual agent harness — not a re-implementation of it — produces a model tuned to that harness. Freezing Qwen3.8-27B and adding a custom RL-trained LoRA adapter per harness (pi, OpenCode, Hermes Agent) lifted Terminal-Bench 2.1 from 39% pass@1 to 55%, while average tokens per task fell from 2.9K to 1.5K — the model reasons less, calls fewer tools, and gets it right first time more often. The post shares the full reproducible recipe: 871 validated RL tasks built from SWE-smith, SWE-Gym and a new Tasksmith generator, TRL's AsyncGRPOTrainer on Hugging Face Jobs, and a merged super-adapter that runs unchanged across all three harnesses. All artifacts are released.

Why it matters: A practical recipe for the gap every agent team hits: the base model is general, your harness is specific — and a small adapter trained in the real loop closes it, cheaper and faster.
