Top Storiescompany claim Anthropic · 2026-11-06

Teaching AI to build scientific models

Anthropic asks what it would actually take for AI to do science, not just fit data. Using the Hodgkin–Huxley model of neurons as a running example, the essay lays out the three jobs of modelling — building a model, constraining it against data, and interpreting it — and shows where LLMs can help: simulation-based inference can test a distribution of plausible models instead of hunting one 'best fit', and agent systems can now propose and refine candidate models on their own. The honest limit: so far these systems mostly rediscover models scientists already know, and interpretation — deciding what a model means — is still the hardest part to hand to a machine.

Why it matters: It's the clearest statement yet of how labs think AI will (and won't) discover new science — model-building as a discipline, not curve-fitting.

Top Storiescompany claim Anthropic · 2026-11-06

The components of agent memory

Claude's memory is the knowledge it keeps between sessions, and Anthropic breaks down how to build it well. The key idea is 'dreaming': using idle time between interactions to re-check notes against session transcripts, fix mistakes, and build linked wiki-style representations. The measurements are striking — on a 350,000-file internal corpus, accuracy on 25 questions rose from 13% to 48% as dreaming scaled from 2 to 128 sessions, and adding a wiki to search saved about 20,000 tokens per question. In a Pokémon Red test, the model that wrote nine focused memory files earned four badges while one that archived everything in 413 files earned one. Letting strong models judge for themselves what is worth remembering beat fixed rules; older models needed explicit prompts to read memory at all.

Why it matters: Agent memory is becoming a measurable engineering discipline — with real numbers behind what to save, when to forget, and how to self-correct.

Frontier Labs & Big Techcompany claim Databricks · 2026-11-06

Read, don't copy: replacing file feeds with OpenSharing — Part 2 (How to Implement)

The companion to Part 1 turns the OpenSharing pitch into a working recipe. A provider shares a Delta table with history enabled; the recipient mounts it as a catalog and reads it as a Structured Streaming source, so checkpoints track what has already been read and each run pulls only new rows — a demo shows the second run writing 200 rows, not 1,200. Change Data Feed adds inserts, updates and deletes when downstream systems need them, and recipients outside Databricks connect through the open protocol with a credentials file. Operationally, adding a customer becomes a grant instead of a new SFTP endpoint or export job.

Why it matters: Yesterday's 'why' becomes today's 'how' — a concrete, checkable recipe for zero-copy data sharing.

Engineering & Researchcompany claim Hugging Face · 2026-11-06

ToyBlocks: A controlled world for robot learning and evaluation

AllenAI's FoundationWorlds is a new open simulation framework for embodied AI, and ToyBlocks is its first world: a robot arm and blocks, with tasks, demonstrations, evaluation metrics and scenes generated automatically. It is built to measure what flashy demos hide — popular action models generalise poorly to new spatial layouts, long-horizon planning still struggles even with strong vision models, and agent systems working through MCP tools behave very differently from direct controllers. The experiments use Molmo 2, ManiFlow, Claude Opus 5.5 and Fable 5.1, with code, data, models and docs all released.

Why it matters: Robot learning needs benchmarks that punish shortcuts — this one is designed to do exactly that, and it's fully open.

Business, Funding & Policycompany claim Databricks · 2026-11-06

How public sector organizations can govern AI at scale

Government teams want to use AI, but every deployment hits the same wall: who can use which models, where does the data go, and what does it cost? Databricks' answer is to centralise governance before scaling adoption, via its Unity Gateway: administrators control which models, coding agents and MCP tools are available and to whom, set spending caps by user or team, and keep every request attributable. Data residency is handled by geography, and smart routing sends routine tasks to cheaper models. It is a vendor's pitch, but the framing — governance as the thing that lets agencies say yes, not the thing that stops them — is the real argument.

Why it matters: The public sector's AI bottleneck is governance, not capability — and this is the clearest statement of the centralised-governance case.

Worth Your Timecompany claim Perplexity · 2026-11-06

AI Accessibility: A Practical Guide to Uses, Tools, and Limits

A thorough, unusually honest guide to AI and accessibility: how AI helps (text-to-speech, dictation, alt text, captions, audio description, plain-language rewrites) and where the tools themselves fall short. It does not hide the failure modes — in a 2026 diary study, 22% of follow-up answers contained false information, and 21% of blind and low-vision users surveyed said an AI mistake had hurt them or someone they know — and it gives practical WCAG-aligned advice for making AI tools themselves accessible, including a Microsoft test where pass rates rose from 12% to 86% with checklists, skills and self-testing.

Why it matters: The rare vendor guide that leads with its own limitations — genuinely useful for anyone building or choosing AI tools.
