Top Storiescompany claim Anthropic · 2026-11-09

Progress on provable inference for large language models

Anthropic released FlashProve, an open-source prototype that uses zkSNARK cryptography to prove a response really came from the claimed model — without revealing the model's weights. On a single H100 GPU it generates proofs at around 110 tokens per second for Llama 4 Maverick, a 400-billion-parameter mixture-of-experts model, with a proof of about 19 MB per 1,024-token request — roughly 70–95x the GPU time of running the model itself. The release includes a technical report, a protocol specification, executable prover and verifier code, and Lean formal proofs, all under Apache 2.0.

Why it matters: The first practical step toward verifiable inference: customers, evaluators and regulators could check they are getting the model they paid for, and it gives AI governance a cryptographic tool instead of a promise.

Top Storiescompany claim OpenAI · 2026-11-09

Control Room: A central system to track and stop model workloads across our research infrastructure

OpenAI built a central control plane over its research infrastructure that tracks every model workload — training runs, evaluations, agents and inference — across data centers, with owner, purpose, resource use, lineage, checkpoints and risk information in one queryable view. The point is the stop: a central kill capability that can halt a single run or a whole cluster, triggered by staff, safety teams or automated monitors. It revokes access tokens, cuts off data, compute and external access, and isolates affected infrastructure. Stop commands propagate in seconds, with the goal of halting workloads within minutes, and the system is drilled regularly and designed to keep working if parts of the infrastructure are down.

Why it matters: OpenAI's own framing is blunt — if you can't stop a model, you can't deploy it safely. This is the final control layer for a misaligned or compromised model, a security incident, or an external oversight demand.

Top Storiescompany claim Anthropic · 2026-11-09

Claude can design electronic prototypes from start to finish

Claude Fable 5.2 designed three electronic devices end to end — a speakerphone, a voice-assistant hub and a handheld virtual pet — from a plain prompt: specifications, schematics, PCB layout and routing, a 3D-printable enclosure, firmware and a parts list, with engineers at Diode Computers only building what Claude directed. All three prototypes worked with no fixes to their circuitry. On Diode's PCB Gym layout benchmark Fable 5.2 scored 69%, up from 25% for Fable 5; on BenchCAD 2.1 it scored 83.5%, nearly double Fable 5.1. Claude even wrote its own PCB router when the built-in one proved too slow, and re-routed whole sections when its own layouts failed.

Why it matters: Frontier models are crossing from software into physical engineering: geometric reasoning, self-correction and real manufacturing checks (design-for-manufacturing, sourcing) done by the model itself — not just code generation.

Closing the AI guidance gap in education

New ThinkYoung research finds a clear guidance gap: 79% of surveyed students use AI for homework or assignments, but only 47% have been shown how. OpenAI's EMEA education partnerships — in Estonia, Slovakia, Greece and Italy — focus on equitable access to frontier tools, jointly designed AI literacy programmes, and ongoing measurement and evaluation. The article argues that as AI gets more capable, teachers become more important, not less, and shares early lessons around clear educational goals, educator preparation, local partnerships, critical AI literacy and credible evidence, including through the OpenAI Learning Lab.

Why it matters: Student AI use is already near-universal; the open question is whether schools teach them to use it well. This is OpenAI's evidence pitch to the governments now writing those rules.

Startups to Watchcompany claim ElevenLabs · 2026-11-09

ElevenAgents Campaign Manager works every contact to an outcome

ElevenLabs launched Campaign Manager for its ElevenAgents voice agents: give it contacts and a desired outcome — a qualified lead, a confirmed appointment — and it runs the campaign toward that outcome, following up over time and carrying context between touches. Contacts enroll by CSV upload or API; channels are phone calls, WhatsApp voice and WhatsApp messaging, with email and SMS coming soon. Campaigns get calling windows, attempt limits with a separate callback cap, per-campaign do-not-call lists that update the moment a contact asks to stop, and disclosures baked into the agent's opening. Analytics run as one funnel per campaign from enrollment to outcome, with a per-contact decision timeline and a needs-attention queue. It was in beta with customers across insurance, banking and ecommerce, and is available now under Campaigns in the Agents sidebar. Early evidence: The AA Ireland's CTO says it now reaches 100% of its daily leads, up from 50–60% with its previous system.

Why it matters: Voice agents are moving from one-off calls to managed campaigns — the tooling question now is orchestration and outcome tracking, not just voice quality.

Engineering & Researchcompany claim Hugging Face · 2026-11-09

Your Experts Have Partners. There's HOPE for Keeping Them Together

Amazon researchers released HOPE (Higher-Order Pruning of Experts), a second-order method for pruning mixture-of-experts models that accounts for how experts cooperate in groups, instead of scoring each expert in isolation. At 50% pruning it beats the best first-order method (REAP) on average rank, and on SWE-Bench Pro a pruned Qwen3.5-35B scored 42.3 — matching the unpruned model's 41.8 while REAP fell to 36.2. The team released a hope package (calibrate, solve, prune) tested on Qwen3, Qwen3-Next, Qwen3.5 and GLM-4.5.

Why it matters: Halving an MoE's experts frees memory for longer context and deeper reasoning — and HOPE shows how to do it without breaking the expert teams that actually do the work.

How Stripe Built Kai on Deep Agents in 1 Week

Stripe's company-wide AI agent Kai, built on LangChain's open-source Deep Agents harness, went from one engineer's week of work to 5,000 users in about four weeks; today 83% of Stripe uses it weekly across more than 60,000 sessions. Deep Agents supplied the tool-calling loop, middleware and state management; Stripe added a virtual S3-backed filesystem, sandboxed code execution and summarization middleware, plus a federated skills library of over 1,000 skills from more than 100 teams, with dynamic tool loading driven by each skill's allowed-tools list.

Why it matters: The clearest production proof yet that an open-source agent harness can carry an enterprise-wide deployment — and a practical playbook for skills-based tool selection at scale. (Post updated November 9.)

Engineering & Researchcompany claim Databricks · 2026-11-09

Accelerate GPU training on Unity Catalog tables with UCDataset

UCDataset, a native PyTorch data loader in beta on Databricks AI Runtime, streams Unity Catalog tables directly to every GPU in a training job — no Parquet export, no copies, no custom sharding code. On a simulated production workload (a 300 GB feature table across 8 NVIDIA H100 GPUs, training a recommendation model) it cut training time 10x versus reading an exported Parquet copy. Governance carries over: jobs read as their own identity, with Unity Catalog permissions applied.

Why it matters: GPUs sitting idle waiting on data is the hidden tax on training runs. Removing the export step attacks that bottleneck directly, with governance intact.

Elith and NVIDIA released a 9-million-persona synthetic dataset (1M records x 9 personas, 50 fields, about 1.5B tokens) grounded in Japan's official demographic and labor statistics plus household finance and financial-literacy data, built with NeMo Data Designer and NeMo SDG-PGMs and shaped with input from Sompo Japan, Tokio Marine, Mizuho and other Japanese financial institutions.
