Top Storiescompany claim Anthropic · 2026-11-25

Migrating our Messages API to Rust

Anthropic moved its Messages API — the primary route customers and its own apps like Claude Code reach Claude — from Python to Rust, after traffic grew roughly 1,800 times since early 2024. Claude wrote the first version of the core request path in about a week, but the hard part was proving parity: the Python API kept changing, Claude's replies are nondeterministic, and the team could not read customer requests. They turned production itself into a verifier, shadowing live traffic, comparing each request stage by stage through fingerprints, and dispatching agents to fix each new kind of difference. The result: about 70% less CPU per request and about 20% faster median time to first token, shipped without downtime or a feature freeze.

Why it matters: A rare inside look at AI rewriting its own production path at scale — and the verification machinery, not the code generation, was the real work.

Top Storiescompany claim Together AI · 2026-11-26

Distilling Kimi-K3.1-Pro into Qwen3.8-27B: Teaching a model a new language

Qwen3.8-27B, a popular small model, could not write Lao — it answered in Thai instead, scoring 5.5 chrF++ on FLORES-200 against Kimi-K3.1-Pro's 47.5. Together distilled the teacher into the student with off-policy distillation: about 3,800 of Kimi's translations as SFT data, trained as a LoRA adapter. The student reached 44.3 — 93% of the teacher — and writes Lao on every test sentence. The catch was forgetting: training on Lao alone collapsed Thai from 46.7 to 5.3; mixing in about 1,000 Thai translations fixed it. All experiments together cost $260. The same recipe also lifted Northern Sotho, Yoruba and Zulu.

Why it matters: A precise, cheap recipe for patching a small model's narrow blind spots without breaking what it already does — the kind of post-training work every team ends up doing.

Sakana Marlin Update: Twilight 2.0 and Midnight 2.0, Two New Modes for Faster and Deeper Research, plus Redesigned Slides

Sakana updated its Marlin research agent with two new modes alongside the original Sunlight: Twilight 2.0, which surveys a topic quickly and broadly (about three times the sources in over an hour less time), and Midnight 2.0, the deepest mode, drawing on roughly seven times the sources and producing reports about five times as long. Top Storiescompany claim Hugging Face · 2026-11-26

Building on Nemotron 3.5 Parse: Open Data, Fine-Tuning, and Faster Inference

NVIDIA and Hugging Face are opening up the work behind Nemotron 3.5 Parse, the compact model that turns page images into structured data for RAG and agents. They released three synthetic training datasets — about 10M chart examples, 1M tables, 7.9M multilingual OCR pages — plus a fine-tuning recipe using SFT followed by GRPO reinforcement learning, which cut looping outputs by about 77% and lifted the overall score from 66.24 to 71.38. A new DFlash2 speculator pushes inference to 20.44 pages per second on one H200, about 6.9 times Parse 2.0. A worked dental-form example takes first-attempt success from 0% to about 96% after SFT and RLVR.

Why it matters: Document parsing is the unglamorous bottleneck for enterprise agents — and here the data, the recipe and the speed are all open.

Top Storiescompany claim Google DeepMind · 2026-11-25

From spectrum to structure: Identifying molecules with Mass Spec Skills for Google Antigravity

DeepMind shipped Mass Spec Skills for Google Antigravity, pairing Gemini's chemical reasoning with established mass-spectrometry tools and public chemical databases. Most measured spectra — about 95% — have never been annotated, because matching a spectrum to a structure by hand takes an expert far too long per sample. The agent runs the chemist's workflow end to end: library lookup, formula deduction, candidate ranking against simulated fragments, cross-checks. On 100 benchmark compounds, accuracy hit about 97% exact match — and the lightweight Gemini 3.8 Flash matched the frontier Gemini 4 models once the skills were added, while using nearly four times fewer generated tokens.

Why it matters: A clean demonstration of the skills pattern: the model does not need to know the chemistry, it needs to drive the tools that do — and the small model wins once it can.

Amazon Transcribe Turbo: more accurate transcripts and natural turn-taking for voice agents, now in public preview

AWS's next-generation speech-to-text model, Transcribe Turbo, is in public preview for streaming (batch is already generally available in English). Word error rate drops about 14% relative in English and about 11% in five other languages, with formatting accuracy up more than 20 points — no API changes needed. Two new voice-agent features: semantic turn-taking, which detects the end of a turn from meaning and prosody rather than silence (responds in about 615 ms with a 5% interruption rate), and runtime ASR hints, which lift menu-routing accuracy from 87% to 97% on customer-service data. Both are free.

Why it matters: Turn-taking is the difference between a voice agent that feels alive and one that keeps cutting people off — and AWS is now shipping it as a commodity feature.

Frontier Labs & Big Techcompany claim OpenAI · 2026-11-24

How iFood scales coding agents across 15,000 repositories

iFood built a Remote Agents Platform that turns engineering tasks into API calls: a prompt goes in, a GPT-5.6 Luna agent works in an isolated sandbox, and the result comes back as a merge request. It has completed more than 25,000 tasks across 15,000 repositories, consuming over 27 billion tokens at about $0.04 per run. Deterministic steps (clone, setup, publish) bookend the agentic work, and Structured Outputs enforce a contract on every result so success can be judged automatically. Its first big user, the internal security platform, more than doubled both the accuracy of its fixes and the number of vulnerabilities resolved.

Why it matters: The pattern here — deterministic scaffolding around an agent, cost-per-run as an architecture decision — is the template for coding agents at real scale.

Startups to Watchcompany claim Together AI · 2026-11-25

Introducing Hurdle: Run agent evals on your API, skills, and docs

Together open-sourced Hurdle, the npm library it built over six months to test how well coding agents (Claude Code, Codex) can actually use its API, SDK and docs. You write tasks as plain prompts, score runs with code checks, guards that block dangerous actions (like provisioning a real GPU cluster), and an LLM judge against a rubric. Sandboxed runs go through a local proxy, Guardian, that swaps in real API keys without ever letting the agent see them. `npx hurdle init` gets a suite running; results open in a viewer with pass rates by task, model and condition.

Why it matters: As agents become the main way developers touch your product, 'does the agent succeed with my docs' is a metric worth measuring — and now there is an open tool for it.

Engineering & Researchcompany claim Lambda · 2026-11-25

Sequence-level RL for diffusion language models

Lambda, Stanford, Renmin and Tsinghua introduced ESPO, a reinforcement-learning method built for diffusion language models, which generate by refining whole sequences rather than token by token. Instead of token-level GRPO, ESPO treats the entire sequence as one action, estimates likelihoods via the ELBO, and stabilizes updates with sequence-length normalization and a robust KL estimator. On LLaDA-8B, Countdown jumps from 18.7 to 81.0 and Sudoku from 15.7 to 86.0; GSM8K rises 75.9 to 82.0 and MBPP 37.8 to 45.4. The paper is accepted to ICLR 2026, with code released.

Why it matters: Diffusion LMs are the most credible alternative to autoregressive generation — and they finally have an RL method shaped to how they actually work.

Engineering & Researchcompany claim Databricks · 2026-11-23

What 5,400 malicious npm Packages Reveal about Software Supply Chain Risk in 2026

Databricks analyzed about 5,400 confirmed malicious npm packages published December 2025 to October 2026. Attacker-created packages are 89% of the set but 0.2% of downloads; compromised legitimate packages are 7% — and over 99% of downloads. The August 2026 worm republished packages through compromised CI pipelines using trusted publishing, not stolen tokens. A distinct class now targets AI development tooling: MCP servers that harvest credentials, packages that rewrite AI assistant configs, and stealers specialized in AI service keys. And removal lags reporting: median takedown is 7 days, with 10% taking over 59 days — 16% of the packages were still installable at analysis time.

Why it matters: The supply chain threat has moved onto AI developers' own tooling — MCP servers and agent configs are now the attack surface.

Worth Your Time

Worth Your Timecompany claim Hugging Face · 2026-11-25

Where scientists draw the line on AI agents

The Allen Institute for AI interviewed 17 scientists about what responsible AI agents in research would actually require, and the answers go well beyond accuracy. They identified five dimensions of scientific trust — validation, privacy, objectivity, accountability, transparency — and a consistent demand: researchers must be able to inspect how an agent reached a result, what it accessed, and what it omitted. Some drew hard lines, like refusing to let AI generate research ideas; others rewrote whole papers after losing track of what was theirs. The piece argues for benchmarks shaped by scientists themselves, testing whether agents follow a field's norms — not just whether they get the answer right.

Why it matters: The most thoughtful thing published this week on agent evaluation — it asks who gets to define trustworthy, and answers: the scientists, not the benchmark makers.
