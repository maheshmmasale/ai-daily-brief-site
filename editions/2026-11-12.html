Top Storiescompany claim Google DeepMind · 2026-11-11

AlphaGenome 2: Deciphering how cells read DNA to better understand disease

DeepMind's next-generation DNA sequence model predicts how genetic variants affect hundreds of human cell types, redesigned as a conditional model that takes a specific cell type or state as input. Trained on more than 107 million individual cell profiles across 68 tissues, it identifies nearly twice as many variants that change gene expression as the original AlphaGenome — 30% vs 16% causal-variant recall on the TenK10K immune-cell dataset. Why it matters: It turns static cell atlases into a predictive engine for human genetics — the kind of model that could change how disease genetics is done, not just benchmarked.

Startups to Watchcompany claim Vapi · 2026-11-11

Agent Loop is here: Better voice agents, every call

Vapi launched Agent Loop in early access (GA planned for early next year): a self-improving harness that builds a voice agent from a plain-language goal, scores every call against it, finds the misses, drafts a fix, tests it in hundreds of simulated conversations, then A/B-tests it on live traffic and rolls back if the number drops. Nothing ships without human approval. The underlying primitives — simulations, traffic splitting, goals, call health, experiments — are coming to the dashboard too.

Why it matters: Voice agents live or die on iteration speed; Vapi is productizing the improvement loop its own forward-deployed team used to run by hand.

Engineering & Researchcompany claim Anthropic · 2026-11-09

Progress on provable inference for large language models

Anthropic released FlashProve, an open-source prototype for provable inference: zkSNARK proofs that a response came from the claimed model, without revealing weights. For a 400B-parameter mixture-of-experts model (Llama 4 Maverick, 4-bit), a single H100 generates proofs at around 110 tokens per second with a proof size of about 19 MB per 1,024-token request — 70 to 95 times the GPU time of running the model on vLLM. The release includes a technical report, protocol spec, executable prover and verifiers, Lean proofs and a visualizer, all under Apache 2.0.

Why it matters: If regulators or evaluators ever need proof of which model served a response, this is the first practical software-only answer — and Claude built much of it.

Worth Your Timecompany claim Mercor · 2026-11-11

What workers want to hand to AI, and what they want to keep

Mercor surveyed workers from its TaskNet occupations about AI and their jobs: across roughly 9,000 task ratings, workers most want to keep human-interaction and high-stakes tasks — mentoring juniors, leading resuscitations, interviewing clients — and hand clerical work to AI. Asked what they'd do with the time saved, 38% would do more of the work they enjoy, 22% the same work at higher volume, and only 15% would work fewer hours. Exposure scores track reported change — except for ER physicians, who report less change and worry than their exposure predicts.

Why it matters: The people training frontier models have a clear view of what should and shouldn't be automated — and it's not the view you'd get from a vendor deck.
