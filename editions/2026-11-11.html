Top Storiescompany claim Google DeepMind · 2026-11-10

Introducing AlphaProof Code: a breakthrough in verifying complex software at depth

DeepMind researchers used a new agentic framework, AlphaProof Code, to formally verify the actual code behind Signal's cryptographic protocol — the first time a widely deployed protocol has been verified down to its implementation by any method, with or without AI. Agents powered by Gemini Carbon generated over six million lines of Lean proof code, with human experts reviewing just 1% of the codebase; the work took four weeks, a task that historically takes expert teams years. The team also reintroduced two real, previously fixed bugs and confirmed the system correctly proved the buggy versions insecure. The verification artifacts are being open-sourced.

Why it matters: Formal verification has been the gold standard nobody could afford at scale. If agents can do it in weeks, the security bar for critical software — messaging, medical devices, power grids — moves for everyone.

Startups to Watchcompany claim Perplexity · 2026-11-11

Web grounding for the Decisions API

Perplexity's Decisions API can now search the web before it answers: adding "mode": "web_grounding" runs a Fast Search query per question and feeds the results in as evidence, so decision models return probabilities informed by current facts. On the public PWS-Decisions benchmark, accuracy rose from 51% to 86%, with typical responses under 400 ms; on 328 true-or-false claims, accuracy went from 56.4% to 97.3%. Each completed search costs $0.001 on top of input tokens, and state can also be a URL pointing the model at a specific page.

Why it matters: Decision models that only know their training data go stale fast. Web grounding at sub-400ms latency makes probability-over-facts a live API primitive, not a research demo.

Engineering & Researchcompany claim Hugging Face · 2026-11-10

Decision Models in Hugging Face

Hugging Face explains and ships support for decision models — models that return a probability distribution over predefined answers instead of generating text. A new `decision` pipeline takes a state plus typed questions (choice, score, or yes/no) and returns structured decisions with confidences, illustrated with a 26B model that routes a support ticket to Billing at 99.8% confidence. The post argues this beats prompting an LLM for JSON on reliability, cost and schema guarantees, and distinguishes decision models from fixed classifiers and zero-shot classification.

Why it matters: Not every problem needs text generation. For routing, triage and scoring, a probability over known options is cheaper, faster and more reliable — and now it is a first-class pipeline.

Engineering & Researchcompany claim Baseten · 2026-11-10

How Baseten and Genspark trained a deep research model to outperform Opus 5.5

Baseten's Applied Research team post-trained Genspark's open Qwen3.5-397B into a specialised Deep Research model that scores 0.759 on Genspark's own quality grader, up from 0.612 for the base model and ahead of Opus 5.5 (0.738), Sonnet 5.5 (0.742) and Kimi K3 (0.724). The recipe split the job in two — reinforcement learning for research breadth, supervised fine-tuning for report writing — with a shaped reward crediting distinct searches and sites read. The model now crawls 5x more sources per query, makes 28.5 tool calls per question versus 7.9, and serves in production on just 2xB200 GPUs per replica with NVFP4 weights.

Why it matters: A detailed, reproducible recipe for the open-model playbook: post-train a big open model on your own evals and beat the closed frontier on your own workload, at a fraction of the serving cost. Scores are on Genspark's own grader, so read them as a vendor claim.

BotPsy: On-Device Personal Assistant with a Sub-Billion-Parameter Agentic Language Model

Tether AI Research's BotPsy is a 0.8B-parameter model built on Qwen3.5-0.8B for on-device agentic work — tool calling, web search, calendar, email and device control — trained in four stages (SFT, multi-teacher distillation, two rounds of RL). It roughly doubles its base model's agentic scores (BFCL v4 23.4 to 44.0, τ-bench 23.2 to 51.5) and beats Qwen3.5-2B-Think, a model 2.5x its size, on five of six agentic benchmarks, while reaching answers with about 3x fewer reasoning tokens. Why it matters: The clearest evidence yet that small, specialised agents can punch far above their weight on-device — and the training recipe is laid out in enough detail to learn from, not just admire.
