Top Storiescompany claim OpenAI · 2026-11-23

OpenAI and Cisco introduce Shirudo

OpenAI and Cisco launch Shirudo — Japanese for 'shield' — an open-source runtime that protects running Linux systems while patches are being prepared. Built on eBPF, it lets security teams test and deploy temporary 'shields' that block the code path an exploit needs while legitimate traffic keeps flowing; in a controlled test, a Codex-generated shield stopped a Java4Shell attempt against a live Elasticsearch service with no restart. Shields are cryptographically signed and verified before loading, with TPM-backed records of what was loaded or removed. The code is BSD 2-Clause licensed, and the goal is to ship tested shields alongside vulnerability disclosures.

Why it matters: Patch windows are the gap attackers live in — a tested, deployable shield that buys days without downtime changes the economics of that gap.

Top Storiescompany claim Anthropic · 2026-11-23

Working with AI

Anthropic interviewed 66 people across 58 enterprise customers — from law firms and manufacturers to hedge funds and publishers — about how AI is actually landing inside their organisations. The headline findings: most AI value comes from expanding output rather than cutting costs; few domestic layoffs were driven by AI, though hiring slowed and offshore contractor use fell; and the highest-value deployments required real reorganisation — broadening roles, redeploying people to bottleneck tasks AI can't do — rather than just dropping tools into existing workflows. Top-down automation bred resistance; bottom-up experimentation stalled on uneven adoption.

Why it matters: The most grounded picture yet of enterprise AI adoption: the technology is the easy part, reorganising around it is where the value — and the friction — lives.

Top Storiescompany claim Databricks · 2026-11-23

What 5,400 Malicious npm Packages Reveal about Software Supply Chain Risk in 2026

Databricks analysed roughly 5,400 confirmed malicious npm packages published between December 2025 and October 2026. The exposure is concentrated: attacker-created packages are 89% of the set but 0.2% of downloads, while compromised legitimate packages are 7% of the set and over 99% of downloads. A distinct class now targets AI development tooling — MCP servers that harvest credentials, packages that rewrite AI-assistant config files, and stealers specialised in AI service keys. And the registry is slow: median 7 days to takedown, with 16% of malicious packages still installable at analysis time.

Why it matters: If you build with AI agents, your supply chain now includes the agent's own tooling — and the report shows attackers have already noticed.

RoboTrack: Point tracking for real-world robotic manipulation

Allen AI releases RoboTrack, a benchmark and training suite for point tracking in robotic manipulation: RoboTrack-Bench has 307 human-annotated videos (2,522 tracks) from DROID, Ego-Exo4D, MolmoAct 2 and EgoVerse, and RoboTrack-15M is 15 million synthetic tracks generated in the MolmoSpaces simulator. Leading trackers score 3 to 6.6 AJ lower on it than on existing robotics benchmarks — the old benchmarks were flattering them. Two new models, RoboTracker and RoboTracker-Warp, are released, and point tracking beat masks and optical flow in a real pipetting task, completing 17 of 20 trials.

Why it matters: Robots that can't reliably track what they're holding can't manipulate it — this shows the benchmarks were overstating readiness, and gives researchers the data to fix it.

Engineering & Researchcompany claim Surge AI · 2026-11-24

Better Judgment, Not Just Better Tool Use: What Nemotron Learned From RL

Surge AI trained NVIDIA's Nemotron 3.5 Super (120B total, 12B active) with reinforcement learning on long-horizon tool-use data, then tested it on Zapier's AutomationBench — 600 real business workflows it never saw in training. End-to-end completion nearly doubled, from 14.8% to 27.5%, and on 66 tasks involving apps entirely absent from training it jumped from 4.5% to 33.3%. The gains came from four habits: finding rules outside the request, treating dead ends as clues, not inventing data to finish, and letting policy beat the request. Cost per completed workflow stayed roughly flat at about $0.49.

Why it matters: The most useful kind of agent result: RL on realistic workflows taught generalisable judgement, not just better scores on familiar tasks.

Engineering & Researchcompany claim Surge AI · 2026-11-24

Coding Agent Harnesses Don't Replace Good Judgment

Surge AI tested 8 coding-agent setups — 5 native, 3 third-party skill packs — on its sudo L7 benchmark of staff-engineer judgement, and found skill packs mostly re-elicit behaviour the model already has: only one third-party setup was Pareto-optimal. The tokens skill packs spend correlate with quality, but the packs didn't make those tokens smarter — and they add cost, attaching to every request and hitting context compaction sooner. The advice: give agents reasoning budget and let them cook, rather than micromanaging them with rules.

Why it matters: A measured counterweight to the skill-pack hype: for trillion-parameter models, reasoning effort beats packaged instructions — and costs less.

Engineering & Researchcompany claim E2B · 2026-11-23

How to Run AI-Generated Code Safely in an AI Agent Sandbox

E2B's guide to running AI-generated code safely: treat model-written code as untrusted and run it in a disposable sandbox with its own kernel, one session per sandbox, no secrets inside, allowlisted egress, no public inbound traffic and a timeout. It argues Docker isn't enough — containers share the host kernel — and walks through Firecracker microVMs, secret injection at the network boundary, and worked examples including a Stripe balance check. The case study: Rogo runs 10,000 to 15,000 concurrent sandboxes for financial firms.

Why it matters: Every agent that writes code is a code-execution service; this is the clearest practical checklist for sandboxing it, with the Copy Fail CVE as the cautionary tale.
