Engineering & Researchcompany claim Anthropic · 2026-11-17

Building strong safety evaluations

Anthropic published a detailed guide to building safety evaluations, drawing on its own experience and on psychology's replication reforms. The recommendations: staff eval teams with both domain and methods experts, run evals in sandboxes with no internet access and real-time monitoring, match the design to the question (observational vs causal), and measure what you actually mean — an 'appropriate response rate' judged against a rubric, not a raw refusal rate, since refusing a person in crisis is itself a failure. Multi-turn simulations matter because harm usually emerges gradually, and evals should run across languages, modalities and product surfaces.

Why it matters: Safety claims are only as sound as the evals behind them, and this is the most candid how-to yet from a lab that lives by its evals.

Engineering & Researchcompany claim Cerebras · 2026-11-17

Cerebras launches disaggregated inference

Cerebras put disaggregated inference into production: GPUs handle prompt processing (prefill) while Cerebras wafer-scale systems handle response generation (decode), where their SRAM-heavy design has the advantage. In a production deployment on the Cerebras Inference Cloud, AMD Instinct GPUs processed prompts and CS-3 systems generated responses, delivering 5x the throughput per megawatt of a CS-3-only setup with no loss in token speed. Early-access partners include AWS, Fireworks AI, Gimlet Labs and General Compute, with broader deployment across hundreds of megawatts planned by the end of 2027.

Why it matters: Inference economics are now the bottleneck for agentic apps, and splitting prefill from decode is a clean architectural win with measured results.

Engineering & Researchcompany claim Databricks · 2026-11-18

How we raised Databricks cluster rate limits by 7.5×

Databricks replaced fixed request-rate limits with adaptive concurrency limits for cluster creation, a long-running operation where one API call kicks off minutes of provisioning across cloud providers. The new system tracks active provisioning workflows in slots, adjusts the slot count from health signals like provisioning time and thread-pool saturation, and adds fair scheduling and bounded queues. The result: 7.5x higher default cluster-creation rate limits, more than 99.99% fewer rate-limit errors, and over a hundred customer-specific overrides removed.

Why it matters: A clear, measured case study in why fixed rate limits fail for long-running work — and a design (slots, health feedback, fair scheduling) that generalizes well beyond Databricks.

Engineering & Researchcompany claim Glean · 2026-11-18

How Glean auto routing determines the right model for every task

Glean explained how its auto routing picks the model for each enterprise task instead of defaulting to the biggest one. About 25% of requests are simple lookups handled by a fast, lightweight path; the rest start on an efficient model and escalate to specialist 'expert' model-and-reasoning combinations only where evaluations show the frontier wins. The routing is backed by an evaluation of 5,000 real enterprise tasks replayed across 37 model-and-reasoning configurations from 11 models, with an agentic judge that reruns calculations and verifies statements. Admins choose Economy, Balanced or Frontier policies, with budget guardrails that step usage down automatically.

Why it matters: Model routing is becoming the quiet infrastructure of enterprise AI — the right model per task beats the biggest model for everything, on both cost and speed.

Worth Your Timecompany claim Together AI · 2026-11-16

Making open models work for long-horizon agentic workloads

Together AI and Ollama wrote a joint deep dive on why long-horizon coding agents are so hard to serve: every turn resends the whole session, so without prefix caching the work grows with the square of the session length. The post walks through both sides — harness choices (loading tools late, timestamps in system prompts, reasoning-effort changes, oversized tool results, subagent design) and engine choices (routing to the node holding the cache, keeping caches alive, splitting prefill and decode) — with concrete numbers throughout. At 100 turns, caching turns 5,050 units of prompt work into 100.

Why it matters: The single best explanation yet of why agentic workloads break naive inference setups, and the practical rules that keep them fast and cheap.
