Top Storiescompany claim OpenAI · 2026-11-05

GPT-6 Cyber: Advancing the frontier of aligned cybersecurity agents

OpenAI introduced GPT-6 Cyber, a model built to find exploitable vulnerabilities and develop fixes while staying inside the scope researchers authorize. It scores 48.5% on ExploitGym, up from 32.1% for GPT-5.6 Cyber, and avoided exploiting out-of-scope services in 99.93% of honeypot tests without deployment prompts — 100% with them. In real campaigns it developed working exploits for 948 Linux kernel privilege-escalation flaws and found 81 previously unknown high-severity Firefox vulnerabilities, with Mozilla already landing fixes. Access runs through Daybreak Red for approved defenders, backed by a $1B Daybreak for Frontline Defenders grant program.

Why it matters: The first cyber model where the alignment story is as concrete as the capability numbers — and the Linux and Firefox results are real vulnerabilities found and patched, not benchmark points.

Top Storiescompany claim Anthropic · 2026-11-05

How scientists use Claude

Anthropic studied 207,000 Claude and Claude Code research sessions to see how scientists actually use AI. Data analysis dominates — about a third of Claude sessions and nearly half on Claude Code — while idea generation and data gathering lag far behind how much researchers value them. Use tracks capability closely: the tasks AI does well get two to four times the use their time share would predict. The report models that existing capabilities, widely adopted, could double research output in some fields; fully automating non-physical work could raise output up to 15-fold in computer science and statistics. About 40% of surveyed researchers expect models to run full research projects on their own by 2030.

Why it matters: The most grounded estimate yet of AI's effect on science — measured from real usage, not surveys alone — and it points to data analysis, not ideation, as where the gains already are.

Engineering & Researchcompany claim Mercor · 2026-11-03

Introducing Physical-Engineering's Last Exam (PLE) with Google Cloud AI Research

Mercor and Google's AI & Infrastructure research team released Physical-Engineering's Last Exam, a benchmark of 85 long-horizon tasks in mechanical, electrical, civil and structural engineering, written and validated by licensed engineers. The best agent configuration, Claude Opus 5.5 in Claude Code, reaches 48.3% on the hidden test set — GPT-6 Astra is next at 45.8% — at an average of 27 million tokens and 63 minutes per task. No frontier model clears 50%: top agents meet 91% of individual constraints on average, but only 41% of designs clear every hard safety check. Twenty-nine tasks, grading tools and the evaluation container are open.

Why it matters: The first benchmark that grades AI on real engineering judgment — and its verdict is that frontier models still can't be trusted with work a licensed reviewer must sign.

Engineering & Researchcompany claim LangChain · 2026-11-03

How We Optimized Browser Use for Deep Agents

LangChain compared seven browser toolsets for its Deep Agents and picked Stagehand from Browserbase, tuning it with LangSmith traces and evals. On WebVoyager's 100 tasks, Stagehand passed 90% for $1.98 total, while Playwright MCP passed 78% for $26.71 — about 13 times the cost and three times slower per task. The wins came from tool design: code mode that runs many browser steps in one call, compact accessibility-tree snapshots instead of screenshots, and a settle-wait fix that cut an 8-second sleep to 0.6 seconds. On HardBench, median agent time fell from 182 to 124 seconds.

Why it matters: Browser use is where agents meet the real web, and this is a rare public write-up of the failure modes and the engineering that actually fixes them.

Engineering & Researchcompany claim Apple · 2026-11-05

Faster Retrieval for Better, More Efficient RAG

Apple researchers tackle maximum inner product search — the retrieval step inside RAG — by training small neural networks to learn the query distribution instead of relying on geometric clustering alone. Their SupportNet reaches 95% routing recall about 1.9x faster than centroid routing, and KeyNet, which rewrites the query toward its likely best match, hits 99% recall in up to 8x less time on a 2.6M-passage Natural Questions index. The work is an ICML 2026 paper, "Amortizing Maximum Inner Product Search with Learned Support Functions."

Why it matters: RAG quality is often a retrieval problem, not a model problem — learned routers could make the same index both faster and more accurate.

A Practical Guide to Security in November 2026

Cognition's guide to the most chaotic few weeks in security history: the $6.34B Binance theft via a tampered installer and a deployment token on an engineer's laptop, the malicious Claude Code extension in Cursor's marketplace that tried to install an SSH backdoor, the PHP2Shell flaw in a runtime behind roughly 70% of websites, a GitLab remote-code-execution flaw, and a certificate authority that set its private key to 4.
