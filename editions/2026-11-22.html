Top Storiescompany claim Together AI · 2026-11-21

Security event: Prompt leakage for requests in June and July of 2026

Together AI is notifying customers about a security incident in which an unauthorized party downloaded stored prompt samples from its serverless inference service. During development of its public GitHub repository xorl, an AI coding agent committed a credential that opened a private Hugging Face repository holding prompt samples from requests to MiniMax M3, GLM-5.2 and Kimi K2.6, mostly from June 24 and July 4, 8 and 14. The party searched the samples for credentials to other services and tested them; five that were tested turned out to be canary credentials from a Dragos security experiment. Together says it has found no evidence of further leakage, is still reviewing what was exposed, and has made zero data retention the default for new accounts, alongside scoped or short-lived credentials, credential checks on all pull requests, and new rules for using AI coding agents.

Why it matters: An AI coding agent caused a real credential leak at an inference provider — the exact failure mode agent-governance posts keep warning about, disclosed with unusual candour.

Frontier Labs & Big Techcompany claim Databricks · 2026-11-19

Introducing Genie Migrate for Serverless

Databricks has launched Genie Migrate for Serverless in beta, an agent that moves jobs and notebooks from classic to serverless compute. It works in three steps — assess the fleet for serverless readiness, translate configs, libraries, Spark settings and incompatible code into reviewable diffs, and apply the change reversibly against a cloned job so the original stays intact. Nothing is applied until a human approves it, and bulk migration for whole fleets runs through the Databricks CLI. Sandbox validation, which would run the migrated workload in isolation and compare output against the classic baseline before applying, is coming next.

Why it matters: The manual translation work that kept fleets on classic compute becomes a review-and-approve step — the practical unlock for serverless adoption.

Frontier Labs & Big Techcompany claim Databricks · 2026-11-19

Genie One Accuracy: Why Curated Agents Need Governed Context

Databricks ran a controlled study on what makes its Genie One entry point answer business questions accurately, using a fictional commercial auto insurer with 125 questions and planted decoys, including a prompt-injected vendor newsletter. Tables alone scored 64%. Curated agents reached 83% and a governed layer of certified Metric Views, Domains and Pages also reached 83% — but at different things. Together they scored 93–95%, with definition questions at 100% and questions needing both documents and data at 97%. A retail repeat showed the governed layer lifting accuracy from 54% to 82%. The lesson: Genie One sometimes answers directly instead of routing to an agent, so definitions must live where every path can reach them.

Why it matters: Measured proof that governed context, not just better agents, is what makes enterprise AI answers trustworthy — with numbers for each layer's contribution.

Engineering & Researchcompany claim Baseten · 2026-11-20

How we secure 100+ clusters across 20+ clouds

Baseten lays out how it holds one security standard across 100+ clusters on 20+ clouds, including neoclouds that often arrive barely hardened. The design rests on four trust boundaries — infrastructure, operating partners, workloads, internal access — with customer builds in microVMs, tenants isolated on the network, each cluster holding its own certificate authority, and data-distribution credentials rotating within an hour. No credential, key or trust root spans more than one cluster, so even a fully compromised cluster cannot reach another one or the control plane. Verification runs from inside and outside every cluster, with a daily perimeter scan, and the post ends with a checklist teams can apply to their own fleets.

Why it matters: The most detailed public write-up of multi-cloud inference security posture — concrete controls, not a compliance slide.

Engineering & Researchcompany claim Fireworks AI · 2026-11-19

Kimi K3.1 Pro vs K3 and Claude Opus 5.5: Should You Switch?

Fireworks compared Kimi K3.1 Pro against K3 and leading models across 21 practitioner-built benchmarks in its Specialized Intelligence Index. K3.1 Pro beats K3 on 14 of 17 shared benchmarks: it completes 15% more legacy-code tasks at 48% lower cost, and resolves 13% more banking support requests than Claude Opus 5.5 — but at $1.51 per resolution versus $0.80, and more than twice the latency. The study is unusually honest about regressions: memory creation drops 22%, contract review falls 64% (LAB Contracts 8.3 to 3.0), and in 64% of inspected coding attempts the model spotted a risk in its reasoning but neither fixed it nor mentioned it. Routing simulations show pairing K3.1 Pro with GPT-6 Astra or GPT-6.1 Sol beats either model alone. The conclusion: assign it useful work, don't replace your existing model everywhere.

Why it matters: A benchmark study that reports the regressions and the costs alongside the wins — and tells you where not to switch.

Four AI Architecture Patterns for Regulated Industries

Databricks' forward deployed engineers set out four patterns for putting agents into regulated enterprises without breaking determinism or auditability: deterministic control with agentic reasoning (the agent investigates the control, it cannot become the control); agentic execution with a deterministic judge; agentic reasoning with human authority at the decision boundary; and agentic execution with bounded autonomy inside an explicit operating envelope.
