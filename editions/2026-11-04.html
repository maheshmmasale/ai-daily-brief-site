Engineering & Researchcompany claim Anthropic · 2026-11-03

Progress on provable inference for large language models

Anthropic's FlashProve is an open-source prototype for provable inference: cryptographic zkSNARK proofs that a response really came from the model a provider claims to be serving, without revealing the weights. For a 400B-parameter mixture-of-experts model (Llama 4 Maverick, 4-bit), a single H100 generates proofs at around 110 tokens per second with a roughly 19 MB proof per 1,024-token request — about 70 to 95 times the GPU time of running the model itself on vLLM. Code, Lean formal proofs and a technical report are released under Apache 2.0.

Why it matters: If inference moves to third-party routers and agents, 'which model actually answered' becomes a trust primitive — and this is the first practical open prototype for proving it.

Engineering & Researchcompany claim OpenAI · 2026-11-02

Scaling Git at OpenAI

OpenAI built Pando, a new Git-compatible backend for its monorepo, after Codex multiplied parallel development to the point where the old system suffered slow fetches and pushes and reliability at times below 90%. Pando coordinates updates per branch independently (causal consistency instead of repository-wide consistency) and reuses packfiles across fetches instead of generating one per client. In production it cut median push latency nearly 5x and now delivers nearly a petabyte of Git data per day, while engineers and agents keep using ordinary Git clients. OpenAI plans to contribute its Git client improvements upstream.

Why it matters: Agent-scale development breaks assumptions baked into developer tools for decades — Pando is the clearest public writeup yet of what has to change underneath.

Engineering & Researchcompany claim LangChain · 2026-11-03

How We Optimized Browser Use for Deep Agents

LangChain compared seven browser toolsets for its Managed Deep Agents, picked Browserbase's Stagehand, and tuned it using LangSmith traces and evals. Stagehand passed 90% of WebVoyager tasks for $1.98 total at a 17.4-second median, against 78% for $26.71 with Playwright MCP. The wins came from code mode (one call running many browser steps), compact snapshots instead of screenshots, and a settle-wait fix that returns an 8-second sleep in 0.6 seconds once the page goes quiet — cutting HardBench median agent time from 182 to 124 seconds.

Why it matters: Browser agents live or die on context efficiency, and this is a rare quantified answer: tool design, not model choice, delivered the 13x cost gap.

Polymorphic Inference: self-tuning inference for open models

Together argues the era of 'static inference' — one tuned config per model, one hardware platform — is over, because open models now arrive faster and more diverse than ever (80% of OpenRouter traffic goes to models released in the last three months) and traffic shapes and hardware keep shifting. Business, Funding & Policycompany claim Glean · 2026-11-04

Why Australia's AI adoption isn't driving impact

Glean's Work AI Index 2026 (Australia) surveyed 1,500 Australian digital workers: 90% use AI at work and report saving roughly 10 hours a week, yet only 10% say AI has significantly improved their organisation's performance — last among the three countries surveyed. 77% report at least one form of 'botshitting' (shipping AI-assisted work they haven't adequately checked), the highest rate surveyed, and workers spend an average of 6.5 hours a week on 'botsitting' — repairing failed AI sessions — 38% of their AI-related time. The report's argument: adoption spread through enthusiasm, but impact has to be designed, with workflows, context, failure training and visible ownership.

Why it matters: The most quotable enterprise finding of the week: the bottleneck is no longer access to AI, it is the work design around it.
