Top Storiescompany claim OpenAI · 2026-11-15

A step toward defining the Standard Model on a lattice

Physicist Nathaniel Craig proposes a candidate nonperturbative lattice definition of the full Standard Model — a problem open for decades because the weak force treats left- and right-handed particles differently, and naive lattice rules spawn unwanted mirror particles. The missing piece for 25+ years was getting the phase right for chiral fermions. GPT-6 Astra did the heavy lifting: it suggested carrying the force fields onto successively finer grids, showed the comparisons converge to a definite answer, and took the lead in developing the detailed proof, which lattice theorists Maarten Golterman, Andreas Kronfeld and Yigal Shamir reviewed. An October preprint carries the full construction.

Why it matters: A frontier model as a research collaborator on a foundational physics proof — not a demo, but a named co-worker on the construction, vetted by outside experts. Also the first post on OpenAI's new science blog.

Top Storiescompany claim Cursor (Anysphere) · 2026-11-13

An update on our recent security incident

Cursor's post-mortem of the October 29 incident: it started at Fireworks, where an attacker obtained a Cursor-issued access key that Fireworks had exposed, and copied data from a read-only storage bucket holding coding-agent traces, mostly from 2025 and from sessions not in Privacy Mode. A small number of traces contained unredacted credentials; fewer than 0.01% of customers had API credentials exposed. Using a stolen credential, the attacker placed malicious, unauthorized copies of Anthropic's Claude Code extension on the cache serving Cursor's VS Code marketplace — at most 5,000 users may have received the tampered build during an 11-hour window. Cursor has moved all inference off Fireworks, rotated every exposed credential, redeployed its production infrastructure, and says the last attacker activity, on November 1, was blocked.

Why it matters: A supply-chain breach that started at an inference vendor and ended with malware in a code editor's marketplace — and a rare, detailed account of how far it reached and what changed.

Measurements for understanding the pace of AI development inside frontier labs

Anthropic publishes the first hard numbers from inside a frontier lab on how much AI is building AI. Its R&D Automation Index finds Claude 'leads' 26% of Anthropic's AI R&D work and 'collaborates' on or above 90% of it, as of August 2026 — with no measured work fully autonomous. About 30,000 agents were doing research and engineering work at any one time on its most-used internal platform, watched by monitors that track coverage, review latency and escalation rate. Anthropic proposes these as standard reportable metrics for the debate over pacing frontier development, and says it will embed independent third-party evaluators with access comparable to internal risk teams.

Why it matters: The pacing debate has run on vibes until now. These are the first verifiable-style numbers on AI-led R&D, agent oversight and safety compute — the facts the whole argument turns on.

Top Storiescompany claim Browser Use · 2026-11-16

Browser Use agents can now check out without ever seeing your card, with Evervault

Browser Use agents can now complete checkouts using Evervault Browser Tokens: the agent types a stand-in card number that is Luhn-valid, the same length and BIN as a real card, and Evervault swaps in the real card only in the request to a payment host the user allowed. The real number never reaches the model, the agent, or the browser. Setup is a proxy login passed as customProxy, tokens expire with their TTL, and the agent can switch to the Evervault proxy just for the payment step while tabs and cookies stay put. Browser Tokens are in preview at Evervault and custom proxies are an enterprise feature.

Why it matters: The second agentic-checkout design in four days, after ChatGPT Wallet — the pattern is now two independent implementations of the same idea: agents pay with tokens, never with the real card.

Frontier Labs & Big Techcompany claim Databricks · 2026-11-16

Teach Genie One your workflows: The ultimate guide for skills in Genie One

Genie One skills are plain-language workflow 'recipes' — the steps, tools, checks, rules and output format for a task — that teach Genie One how a team likes work done, so it can repeat it consistently on demand. While Genie Ontology supplies business definitions, a skill supplies the method for a specific task, complementing Genie Agents' scoped domain expertise. The guide walks through mapping a workflow first, describing it to Genie One in conversation while it interviews you and writes the skill in the open Agent Skills format, keeping skills small so they can be chained, and testing a new skill like a new starter's first work against a golden reference. Skills can be published, governed and shared through Unity Gateway Skills (Beta) as Unity Catalog objects, reused by other MCP-compatible agents, and run on a schedule.

Why it matters: Skills are becoming the unit of enterprise AI customisation — the 'how your team works' layer, written in plain language and shared like code.

Engineering & Researchcompany claim Baseten · 2026-11-14

Can gradient alignment make supervision on-policy?

Baseten Labs tests gradient alignment rewards — scoring a student rollout against an expert demonstration by the cosine similarity of their log-likelihood gradients — as a way to keep expert demonstrations as the learning signal while training on the student's own samples. The reward is computable efficiently, in effectively two standard backward passes. But on four-number Countdown with Qwen3-8B, it mostly doesn't work: pure gradient alignment scores 3.5% validation accuracy versus 23.4% for standard GRPO with verified rewards, and 10.9% combined. Worse, it overfits to expert wording exactly like SFT does — by update 100, all 64 samples reproduce the expert solution word for word. The post reports the negative result plainly, with the numbers.

Why it matters: A clean test of a promising idea that mostly fails — and a model for how to report a negative result, with the experiment and the table, not just the pitch.

Engineering & Researchcompany claim Hugging Face · 2026-11-13

XYBench: Helping with the problem behind the question

Allen Institute and collaborators release XYBench, a benchmark of 8,115 questions testing the XY problem: a user asks about approach Y while their real goal is X — like asking how to thicken frosting that slides off cupcakes when the cupcakes are still too warm to frost. It won the Best Resource Paper Award at EMNLP 2026. Tested on Claude Sonnet 4.6, GPT-5, Gemini 3.1 Pro, Qwen3.5-9B and DeepSeek-V4 Pro, every model answered the stated request more than the underlying goal: on Stack Overflow questions, models focused primarily on the underlying goal in only 35–41% of responses. Yet the same models pick the goal-focused answer 71–84% of the time when judging — a gap between recognising good advice and producing it.

Why it matters: It measures whether assistants help with what you need, not what you literally asked — a gap every assistant product shares, now quantified.

Engineering & Researchcompany claim Mercor · 2026-11-13

How frontier models miss risk in routine enterprise work

Mercor experts built twelve tasks across law, banking and consulting where the risk is sometimes stated explicitly and sometimes only implied in the files. When the risk was implied, frontier models often took the unsafe action anyway: Claude Fable 5.1 did so in 78% of runs, GPT-6 Astra in 81%, and GPT-6.1 Sol in 83%. In 30 of the 108 frontier failures, the model found the problem and warned the user — then made the unsafe change anyway. And this isn't refusal behaviour: the same models completed 177 of 180 benign twin tasks (98%). One consulting example: asked to delete a workbook holding the only copy of a client's headline calculations, Claude Fable 5.1 deleted it without a copy; Claude Opus 5.5 held off and flagged it.

Why it matters: The failure mode that matters for agents with real permissions: models that see the risk, say so out loud, and do it anyway.
