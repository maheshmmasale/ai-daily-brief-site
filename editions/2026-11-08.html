Top Storiescompany claim Databricks · 2026-11-06

Read, don't copy: replacing file and API feeds with OpenSharing — Part 1 (The Why)

Most teams still ingest customer data over SFTP file drops or API pulls — each one a pipeline to babysit, with fragile incremental loads, backfills that need the customer to re-export, and a duplicate copy to store. OpenSharing, an open protocol built on Delta Lake and Iceberg, flips that: you read the customer's table in place, pull only what changed via its change data feed, and turn backfills into a query instead of a favour. The customer shares a table they already keep, adds recipients with a grant, and can audit every access in system tables instead of trusting on faith.

Why it matters: Customer ingestion is the least glamorous, most failure-prone plumbing in data engineering — and this is the open, zero-copy alternative, readable even without Databricks.

SageMaker Inference now supports Multi-Instance GPU to maximize GPU utilization for model endpoints

Deploying a model to a GPU endpoint used to mean booking a whole accelerator, even if your model needed 14 GB of a 40 GB A100 — eight models max on an eight-GPU instance, and no way to buy a fraction of a newer card. Engineering & Researchcompany claim Databricks · 2026-11-05

From Delta Sharing to OpenSharing: what changed, what's next

Delta Sharing, the open protocol Databricks contributed to the Linux Foundation, has evolved into OpenSharing — announced in June 2026 — extending the same zero-copy model from tables to agent skills, AI models and unstructured data, with Iceberg REST Catalog support and on-premises reach via storage partners. The protocol is now a Linux Foundation AI & Data project (Apache 2.0, on GitHub), existing Delta Sharing clients stay compatible, and new capabilities include SecureConnect (generally available), Global Distribution (private preview) and Genie Agent Sharing (beta).

Why it matters: Sharing is quietly becoming the plumbing for agentic AI — this is the map of what the standard now covers and what is still on the roadmap.

Engineering & Researchcompany claim Databricks · 2026-11-05

Push-based CDC into Unity Catalog with Zerobus Ingest and Debezium

Getting PostgreSQL changes into the lakehouse usually means polling the database or running a Kafka cluster just for transport. Databricks and NVIDIA's new open-source Debezium Server sink pushes change events straight into Zerobus Ingest, which writes them into Unity Catalog Delta tables — no Kafka in the middle. NVIDIA contributed an 'envelope mode' that lands the full change event with idempotency keys and durable acknowledgement before offsets advance; the sink ships in Debezium Server 3.7.

Why it matters: CDC is unglamorous infrastructure, but removing Kafka from the path is a real operational saving for teams whose only destination is the lakehouse.
