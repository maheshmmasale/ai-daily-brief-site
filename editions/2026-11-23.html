AI Daily Brief — Monday, November 23, 2026

Top Storiescompany claim Anthropic · 2026-11-20

LLMs are making formal software verification feasible at a much larger scale

Anthropic reports that Claude proved 239 entry points across 123 OSS-Fuzz libraries, generating roughly 180 million lines of Rocq proofs, and in the process found real, long-hidden bugs — including a rediscovery of CVE-2026-56132. The post argues that LLM-written proofs make formal verification practical at a scale humans never reached, and proposes folding proof-writing into the normal development loop so code ships with machine-checked guarantees.

Why it matters: If proof-writing becomes cheap, 'verified' could become a real property of shipped open-source code rather than a research demo.

Top Storiescompany claim Thinking Machines Lab · 2026-11-23

Defeating Nondeterminism in LLM Inference

The same prompt can return different outputs depending on what else shares its batch, because batching changes the order of floating-point summations. Thinking Machines Lab traces the problem to batch-dependent numerics and shows how to make inference batch-invariant — the same input always producing the same output, regardless of batch composition — arguing reproducibility should be a first-class property of serving stacks.

Why it matters: Flaky evals and unrepeatable benchmarks partly come from this; batch invariance makes model behaviour actually repeatable.

GPT-6.2 Astra vs. Sol: Features, Pricing & Security

Straiker's write-up of OpenAI's GPT-6.2 launch (November 17): Astra priced at $8 input / $40 output per million tokens against Sol at $2 / $10, with Astra leading on benchmarks and Sol positioned as the fast, cheap tier. OpenAI also raised its cyber-risk ceiling to 'Critical' for the release. Straiker adds its own angle — prompt-injection and MCP data-leakage controls for agents built on the models.

Why it matters: The first public pricing and capability split for GPT-6.2, and the agent-security controls are what enterprises actually need before adopting it.

Top Storiescompany claim Baseten · 2026-11-20

Meet bison-1 and groundhog-1, frontier American decision models

Baseten releases two open-weight 'decision models' built on a Gemma 4 backbone — bison-1 at 31B parameters and groundhog-1 at 26B — with 128k context, trained to output calibrated probability judgments rather than chat. Bison-1 tops the Artificial Analysis Decision Index, first of 19 models with a score of 76 and a Brier score of 0.14. Both are available on Baseten at $0.10 input / $0.035 output per million tokens.

Why it matters: A small model purpose-built to output trustworthy probabilities is a different bet than another general chatbot — and it is open weights.

Frontier Labs & Big Techcompany claim OpenAI · 2026-11-22

Perplexity and Intelligence

OpenAI argues that log-perplexity on a fixed text corpus is the cleanest shared yardstick for intelligence: GPT-3.5 sat near 0.99, GPT-4 near 0.91, today's models are below 0.8, and a 0.68–0.7 window lies ahead. The essay proposes common measurement and shared limits on the road to superintelligence, framing perplexity as the metric the industry should converge on.

Why it matters: Read it as a policy signal rather than a product launch — OpenAI's framing for how the industry should measure, and govern, progress.
